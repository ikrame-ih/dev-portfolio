01 · the letter

Hello — I’m Ikrame, and I came to development by a slightly different path.

Before software, I worked as an executive assistant. I managed calendars, visits, and internal tools, and I sat close to the company’s day-to-day — and to its clients. A lot of the work was sorting information, coordinating people, and solving problems that were not always well defined.

That still shapes how I build. I want the whole problem, how what I build will be used, and what can get simpler — not only the technical slice. That is why many of my projects start from problems I already know well: importing information, reconciling data, or turning manual processes into clearer flows.

I am focused on backend and AI now, working with FastAPI, async SQLAlchemy, Pydantic, pytest, and systems that use agents.

When I work with agents, context is the foundation. I keep the architecture, decisions, rules, and project state in Obsidian, so the agents and I start from the same information.

I also break the work into small tasks, each with a clear goal and milestones that mark the progress of every stage. From there I bring in different agents by job, and LangGraph to orchestrate them: one may implement, another test, another review. I am not trying to make every agent do everything. I want each one to have a clear role, the right context, and a well-defined task.

In the end, the way I work joins two things that have always been connected for me: understanding people’s problems well, and building systems that help solve them more clearly and efficiently.

Orca

Who steps in, and when

Orca is the layer that coordinates those agents and decides who enters at each stage. It does not replace the project rules. It uses them.

Product

The model, with a limited margin

When AI touches data, it extracts into JSON with a schema. That payload is validated first; if it fails, it never continues to PostgreSQL. How amounts are interpreted or calculated lives in code, under tests — not in the model.

AI does the part it is useful for. The rest is the system’s rules.
